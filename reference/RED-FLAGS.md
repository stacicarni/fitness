# Red Flags

Read this once, today. It's short.

## 🚨 Call your provider or go to the ER now

Postpartum emergencies are frequently dismissed as "normal new-mom stuff." They are
not. Any of these, at any time in the first months:

- **Bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour**, or clots bigger than a golf ball
- **Fever over 100.4°F**, or foul-smelling discharge
- **Severe headache that doesn't respond to medication**, vision changes, spots or
  flashing lights, or new swelling in the face/hands
  → **postpartum preeclampsia can occur up to 6 weeks after delivery and is a
  medical emergency.** It does not care that the birth went well.
- **Chest pain or shortness of breath**
- **Calf pain, swelling, redness, or warmth in one leg** — possible clot
- **Fainting, or a racing heart at rest**
- Incision/tear site that reopens, oozes, or becomes hot and red
- Inability to urinate, or no bowel movement with pain
- **Thoughts of harming yourself or the baby**

**Postpartum Support International:** 1-800-944-4773 · text 800-944-4773
**988** Suicide & Crisis Lifeline · **911**

## 🛑 Stop the session immediately

- Urine or stool leaking
- Vaginal heaviness, dragging, or a bulging sensation
- Pelvic, pubic bone, or SI joint pain
- Bleeding that starts or increases during exercise
- Abdominal doming or coning that won't resolve when you shorten the range
- Dizziness, nausea, or vision changes
- Sharp pain anywhere

Then apply the [symptom traffic light](../plan/PROGRESSION-RULES.md#rule-4--the-symptom-traffic-light-).

## ⚠️ Book a pelvic floor PT

Not urgent, but don't wait months:
- Any leaking at all, at any point
- Heaviness or bulging at the end of the day
- A diastasis gap that isn't narrowing by ~3 months, or one you can't generate
  tension across
- Pain with intercourse
- A pelvic floor you can't feel contract, or can't feel release
- Low back or hip pain that isn't improving

**Ask at your 6-week visit even if none of the above apply.** In the US it's usually
opt-in; in France and much of Europe every postpartum woman gets it by default. You
have to ask for it here. Kaiser and Sutter both have pelvic health PT in the East Bay,
and it is typically covered.

## 😴 The fatigue that isn't the baby

At the 6-week visit, ask for **CBC, ferritin, TSH, and vitamin D.**

- **Low ferritin** is very common after delivery and feels exactly like new-parent
  exhaustion. It also blunts every training adaptation you're working for.
- **Postpartum thyroiditis** affects roughly 5% of women, usually shows up at 2-6
  months, and gets missed constantly because every symptom overlaps with having a
  newborn.

If energy isn't improving by month 3, that's a lab question, not a willpower question.

## 🧠 Mood

Baby blues peak around day 5 and lift by ~2 weeks. **You're past that window.** If
things feel heavy now, that's worth naming to someone — postpartum depression and
anxiety are common, treatable, and not a character issue. Exercise helps and is not
a substitute for care.

Section E of the [intake survey](../intake/INTAKE-SURVEY.md) screens for this. Answer
it honestly; nobody reads it but you and whoever you choose to show.
