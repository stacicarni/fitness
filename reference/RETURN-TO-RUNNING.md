# Return to Running

You've run a marathon. That makes this harder, not easier — the instinct is to treat
running as the baseline you return to, when postpartum it's the most demanding thing
in the plan and belongs near the end.

## Why the wait

Running is ~2.5-3× bodyweight of impact, landing on one leg, ~160-180 times a minute.
At 12 weeks that's ~2,000 single-leg landings through a pelvic floor and abdominal
wall that are still remodeling. Connective tissue is still relaxin-affected for months
(longer while breastfeeding). The cost of going early isn't soreness — it's stress
incontinence or prolapse that takes a year to walk back.

The current consensus guideline (**Goom, Donnelly & Brockwell, 2019** — the standard
reference in postpartum sports medicine) is: **no return to running before 12 weeks
postpartum**, and then only after passing a load test. Your window is **Nov 9 at the
absolute earliest**, more likely late November.

## The run-readiness test — run it in PP week 13 (~Nov 9)

Do it fresh, not after a workout. **Every item must be completed with no urine
leaking, no vaginal heaviness or dragging, no pain, and no abdominal doming.**

### Part 1 — Endurance & strength
| # | Test | Standard | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk | 30 min brisk, no symptoms | [ ] |
| 2 | Single-leg balance, eyes closed | 10 sec/side | [ ] |
| 3 | Single-leg calf raise | 20/side | [ ] |
| 4 | Single-leg bridge | 20/side | [ ] |
| 5 | Single-leg sit-to-stand | 20/side | [ ] |
| 6 | Side plank, from feet | 30 sec/side | [ ] |
| 7 | Single-leg wall sit | 60 sec/side | [ ] |

### Part 2 — Impact
| # | Test | Standard | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Jog in place | 1 min | [ ] |
| 9 | Forward bounds | 10 | [ ] |
| 10 | Hop in place, one leg | 10/side | [ ] |
| 11 | Single-leg running-man (opposite arm/leg drive) | 10/side | [ ] |

### Part 3 — Screens
- [ ] No leaking during **any** item above, including the day after
- [ ] No heaviness/bulging during or in the 24 hours after
- [ ] Diastasis: able to generate tension, no doming under load
- [ ] Pain-free
- [ ] Lochia fully stopped for 4+ weeks
- [ ] At least 12 weeks postpartum
- [ ] Cleared by your provider

**All checked → start the walk/run ladder in [Phase 3](../plan/phase-3-build.md).**
**Any unchecked → don't start. Train the specific failure and retest in 3-4 weeks.**

### If you fail an item, train that item
| Failed | Train |
|---|---|
| Single-leg calf raise | Double → single calf raises daily, 3 × 15 |
| Single-leg bridge / sit-to-stand | Step-ups, split squats, glute bridge marches |
| Balance | 30 sec/side while brushing teeth, twice a day |
| Any impact item | Back down the impact ladder one rung, hold 2 weeks |
| Leaking | **Pelvic floor PT.** Not more kegels on your own. |

## The walk/run ladder

Full progression in [Phase 3](../plan/phase-3-build.md). The principles:

1. **Walk/run, not run.** Walk intervals are load management, not weakness.
2. **Two runs a week, maximum**, for the first 8 weeks. Never back to back.
3. **Easy only.** Full sentences, or you walk. No pace goals until 2027.
4. **Progress duration only.** Pace is a 2027 conversation.
5. **The 24-hour rule.** Symptoms the next day mean the last step was too big. Repeat
   the week; don't advance.
6. **10% rule** on weekly volume, once you're running continuously.
7. Empty your bladder first, but **do not** habitually "just in case" pee — it trains
   urgency.
8. Run after a feed or pump.
9. **Get a sports bra refitted.** Your chest is not the size it was, and it won't be
   this size in six months either. High-impact, current size.
10. **New shoes.** Pregnancy can permanently change foot size and arch. Get gait-fitted
    — Fleet Feet or Transports in the East Bay. Treat it as a fresh fitting, not a
    repeat of what you wore in 2013.

## Breastfeeding & running
- Relaxin and lower estrogen persist while lactating — tissue is more lax, so ramp
  slower than pre-pregnancy instinct says.
- Fuel before running. Running in a deficit while lactating hits supply first.
- Extra fluid: roughly 16-24 oz beyond your normal intake around a run.
- Nipple friction is real. Lanolin or a barrier balm before, not after.

## Realistic 2027 arc
| When | Where you could be |
|---|---|
| Jan 2027 | 20-25 min continuous, easy |
| Mar 2027 | First 5K back — no time goal |
| Jun 2027 | 10K, comfortable |
| Sep-Oct 2027 | Half marathon, if you want one |
| 2028 | Marathon conversation |

Slower than you want. Almost exactly as fast as it actually goes for experienced
runners who do this well — and dramatically faster than for the ones who start in
week 8 and spend six months managing symptoms.
