# Nutrition

**You're breastfeeding and pumping — all breastmilk.** That sets everything below.
When you wean in December, most of these numbers change; that transition has its own
file: [`WEANING-AND-RETURN-TO-WORK.md`](WEANING-AND-RETURN-TO-WORK.md).

## The single most important rule right now

**You are not in a fat-loss phase.** Not at 3 weeks. Not at 8 weeks.

Your body is healing a placental wound roughly the size of a dinner plate, rebuilding
blood volume, possibly producing 750-900 ml of milk a day, and running on broken sleep.
Underfeeding on top of that produces exactly what you don't want: stalled healing, low
supply, wrecked energy, no training adaptation, and — because your body will protect
milk production first — you end up losing muscle instead of fat.

Body composition is a **2027 project**, and it will work far better then, on a
recovered system with 6 months of strength training behind it. Through 2026 the food
goal is one word: **enough.**

## Priority order

1. **Enough total calories** — especially if breastfeeding
2. **Protein** — healing, muscle, satiety, milk
3. **Fluid**
4. **Micronutrients** — iron, vitamin D, calcium, choline, DHA, B12
5. **Timing / meal structure**
6. Everything else people argue about on the internet

## Calories

Estimated maintenance ≈ **bodyweight (lb) × 14-15** at your current activity, then
**+450-500 kcal/day** for full milk production.

**Your floor is about 2,000 kcal/day, and it is a floor, not a target.** Going under it
while producing a full supply hits milk first, then muscle, then mood — in that order.

Cap any weight change at **~1 lb/week** while lactating. Faster than that is the single
most common cause of a supply drop, and it isn't fat coming off any faster anyway.

## Protein — the one number worth tracking

**Target: 0.7-0.9 g per lb of bodyweight per day.** For most women that lands at
**110-150 g/day.**

That is more than it sounds like and almost certainly more than you're getting on a
day of one-handed eating. Protein is what turns your training into muscle instead of
just fatigue, and postpartum tissue repair raises the requirement above baseline.

**Anchor it: 30-40 g at four points in the day.** Don't try to hit it with dinner.

| Anchor | Easy versions |
|---|---|
| Morning | Greek yogurt + protein powder, 3 eggs + cottage cheese, protein shake |
| Midday | Rotisserie chicken, canned tuna/salmon, leftover dinner protein, deli turkey |
| Afternoon | Shake, cottage cheese, edamame, protein bar, jerky |
| Evening | Whatever dinner is + an extra serving of the protein |

**One-handed, no-prep, 20g+ protein** — keep these visibly stocked:
Greek yogurt cups · cottage cheese · string cheese ×2 · hard-boiled eggs ·
premade shakes (Fairlife, OWYN) · jerky · roasted edamame · protein bars ·
tuna packets · rotisserie chicken pulled into a container on Sunday

**Protein powder is fine while breastfeeding.** Whey isolate or a plain pea protein;
skip anything with herbal "blends."

## Fluid

- **Your target now: 3.5-4 L/day** (baseline ~2.5-3 L, plus about a litre for milk)
- Add 500-750 ml around any training session
- Add more around pumping sessions specifically — that's when you'll notice it
- Back to ~2.5-3 L once you've weaned

Practical: a 32 oz bottle at every feeding station. Drink one **during** every feed —
thirst hits hard mid-letdown and that's the easiest habit to attach it to. Dark urine
or a headache by mid-afternoon usually means you're behind.

## Micronutrients that actually matter here

| Nutrient | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | Blood loss + pregnancy depletion. Low ferritin = exhaustion + no training adaptation | Red meat, lentils, spinach + vitamin C. Supplement only if labs say so. |
| **Vitamin D** | Widely low; bone density matters more at 40 | 1,000-2,000 IU/day. Also passes into milk. |
| **Calcium** | Lactation pulls from your bones | 1,000-1,300 mg/day |
| **Choline** | Requirement is *higher* while lactating than pregnant; most prenatals underdose it | Eggs (yolks), liver, soy |
| **DHA/omega-3** | Infant brain development + your mood | Fatty fish 2×/wk, or 300 mg DHA |
| **B12** | Fatigue; critical if vegetarian/vegan | Prenatal or animal foods |

**Keep taking your prenatal** the whole time you're breastfeeding. It's not optional
during lactation.

## Plate template — when you don't want to think

Every meal, in this order:
1. **Palm-plus of protein** (30-40 g) — build the plate around this
2. **Two fists of vegetables or fruit** — whatever's easy, frozen counts
3. **A cupped hand of starch** — rice, potato, oats, bread. Carbs are not the enemy;
   they're what makes milk and fuels training.
4. **A thumb of fat** — olive oil, avocado, nuts

## Around training

- **Don't train fasted.** Even 20 g of protein and a banana beforehand.
- **Eat within an hour after.** Protein + carbs.
- **Feed or pump before training** — comfort, and it protects supply.
- Training in a deficit while lactating hits supply first. If your supply dips in a
  week you trained hard, look at food before you look at the training.

## Caffeine & alcohol

- **Caffeine:** ≤300 mg/day (about 2-3 cups) is considered fine while breastfeeding.
  Watch it especially once you're back at work — that's when it quietly doubles.
  Cut it off ~8 hours before you intend to sleep — with sleep this fragmented,
  protecting the quality of what you *do* get matters more than usual.
- **Alcohol:** if breastfeeding, roughly 2 hours per standard drink before feeding.
  It also degrades sleep quality noticeably, which is the more relevant cost right now.

## Supplements: worth it / not

**Worth it:** prenatal (continue), vitamin D, omega-3, magnesium glycinate at night
(sleep + cramps), protein powder as a convenience.

**Wait, then revisit:** creatine — genuinely good for you and well-supported for women,
but the lactation data is thin. **Put it on the January list**: once you've weaned,
3-5 g/day is one of the better-evidenced things you can add, and it lines up exactly
with your real build month.

**Skip:** anything marketed as "postpartum weight loss," detox teas, appetite
suppressants, fat burners. All of it is contraindicated while breastfeeding and most
of it is contraindicated generally.

## Logistics — the actual bottleneck

The barrier at 3 weeks postpartum is never knowledge. It's that food has to already be
edible, one-handed, at 2pm, when you haven't sat down.

- **One 45-min Sunday block:** cook 2 lb of a protein, roast a tray of vegetables,
  make a pot of rice or potatoes. That's 4-5 assembled meals.
- **Accept every meal train offer.** Ask for protein specifically.
- **Grocery delivery**, standing weekly order.
- **A "snack station" wherever you feed and pump** — shelf-stable protein, a water
  bottle, restocked nightly. Drink the bottle during the session; thirst hits hardest
  mid-letdown, which makes it the easiest habit to attach to.
- Frozen vegetables, pre-cooked rice, canned fish, rotisserie chicken. None of this is
  cheating. Cooking from scratch is not a nutritional requirement.
