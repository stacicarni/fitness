# Phase 0 — Reconnect
### PP weeks 4-6 · Mon Sep 7 → Sun Sep 27, 2026

**Goal of this phase:** not fitness. Restore the pressure system — breath, pelvic
floor, deep core, and posture — so that everything you load onto it from Phase 1
onward has something to attach to. Three weeks of this is what makes month 3 fast
instead of frustrating.

**No clearance needed for anything in this phase.** Everything here is what a pelvic
health PT would give you at 3 weeks.

### The hard stops until Sep 28
❌ Running, jumping, jogging with the stroller
❌ Crunches, sit-ups, full planks, bicycles, V-ups, mountain climbers
❌ Anything heavier than the baby + car seat
❌ HIIT, bootcamp, "postpartum shred" anything
❌ Deep squats under load, wide-leg / open-chain leg work
❌ Peloton bike, until the gates in Week 6 below are met

### What you actually do
✅ **The Reset** — 12 minutes, daily
✅ **Walking** — the primary "cardio," ramped deliberately
✅ **Strength seeds** — from Week 5, tiny and unimpressive on purpose
✅ **Peloton app** — postnatal, restorative yoga, meditation, stretching only

## The Reset (daily, ~12 min)

Do this every day of Phase 0, including rest days. It is the whole phase. On the
worst day, do items 1-3 and stop — that is still a win.

**1. 360° breathing — 10 breaths (3 min)**
Lie on your back, knees bent, one hand on ribs, one on belly. Inhale through the
nose: ribs widen sideways *and* back, belly softens, pelvic floor releases downward.
Exhale through pursed lips: everything returns without you jamming it in.
> *Cue: think "the breath opens the umbrella on the way in."* Do not suck the belly in.

**2. Connection breath — 10 reps (3 min)**
Same position. **Inhale** to expand and let the pelvic floor release. **Exhale** and
gently lift the pelvic floor (as if stopping gas, then urine) *and* feel the low belly
draw in behind it. Hold 3 seconds. **Fully release** on the next inhale.
> The release matters as much as the lift. A pelvic floor that can't let go is as
> dysfunctional as one that can't grip — and it's more common after pushing.
> **Effort level: 3 out of 10, not 10.**

**3. Pelvic floor sets (2 min)**
- 10 × quick flicks (1 sec on, 1 sec off)
- 5 × long holds — start at 3 sec, add 1 sec per week, target 10 sec by Week 6
- Rest 30-60 sec between sets. Full release between every rep.
- Do these once daily. More is not better; the pelvic floor fatigues like any muscle.

**4. Mobility circuit — 1 round (4 min)**
| Movement | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow | 8 slow | Spine after hours of feeding-hunch |
| Supported chest opener over a block, lengthwise along the spine | 60 sec | Undo the forward round |
| Thread-the-needle | 5/side | Thoracic rotation |
| Glute bridge — exhale up, PF lift at the top | 10 | Reintroduce glutes |
| Heel slides — exhale, slide one heel out, keep low back still | 8/side | Deep core without doming |
| Neck / upper trap stretch | 30 sec/side | Feeding neck |
| Wrist + thumb stretch | 30 sec/side | De Quervain's prevention — very common at 3 wks |

**5. Posture & mechanics — all day, not a set**
- **Car seat:** hinge at the hips, exhale as you lift, get it close to your body. Never
  round-and-yank. This is the single most common re-injury moment in month 1.
- **Feeding:** bring baby to breast, not chest to baby. Pillow under your elbow.
- **Standing:** ribs stacked over hips. Not butt-tucked, not ribs-flared.
- **Getting out of bed:** roll to your side, push up with the arm. No sit-ups out of bed.

## Walking ramp

Flat only. Ohlone Greenway is right there and is ideal — paved, flat, shaded, and you
can turn around at any point. Treadmill at 0% is a fine substitute on bad-weather days.

| PP Week | Dates | Per walk | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | Sep 7-13 | 10-15 min | 1-2×/day | Conversational. Stop before you want to. |
| **5** | Sep 14-20 | 15-20 min | 1-2×/day | Can add gentle grade. Still flat-ish. |
| **6** | Sep 21-27 | 20-30 min | 1×/day + a short second | One continuous 30 min by Sep 27 = the goal |

**The walking rule:** if bleeding increases, turns pinker/redder, or you feel heaviness
or dragging in the vagina *during or after* — that walk was too long. Drop back to the
last comfortable duration and hold it for 3-4 days before advancing again. This is
feedback, not failure. It is the single most reliable dose-meter you have.

**Carrier vs. stroller:** stroller loads your pelvic floor less than wearing the baby.
In Phase 0 default to the stroller for anything over 15 minutes.

## Strength seeds — from Week 5 (Sep 14)

2× per week. Two sets of each. **RPE 3-4 out of 10** — it should feel almost pointless.
The purpose is neuromuscular, not muscular: re-teaching the pattern of *exhale on
effort, pelvic floor lifts with the load.*

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sit-to-stand from a chair | 2 × 8 | Exhale up. No hands if easy. |
| Wall push-up | 2 × 10 | Ribs down, don't let the low back sag |
| Band pull-apart | 2 × 12 | Lightest band. Shoulder blades, not arms |
| Standing hip abduction | 2 × 10/side | Hand on wall |
| Bird dog — opposite arm/leg, low and slow | 2 × 6/side | Stop if the belly domes |
| Calf raise, both feet | 2 × 12 | Quiet groundwork for running later |

**Watch for doming** on bird dog: if a ridge tents up the midline, shorten the range
until it doesn't. That threshold *is* your current training limit, and it moves fast.

## Peloton app in Phase 0

Search these, in this order of usefulness right now:
- **Postnatal** collection — start here, it is built for exactly this
- **Restorative / Slow Flow yoga**, 10-20 min — avoid anything with plank, chaturanga,
  boat, or deep twists
- **Stretching**, 5-10 min — "Post-Natal Stretch," "Neck & Shoulders," "Lower Back"
- **Meditation**, 5-10 min — genuinely part of the program, not filler. Sleep-deprived
  nervous systems don't recover well, and recovery is the whole phase. The cushion is
  already there.

**Skip for now:** all Strength, all Bootcamp, all Core, all Power Yoga, all Cardio.

## Week 6 gate: the Peloton bike (Sep 21+)

You may add **10 min, low resistance, upright-comfortable** only when **all** are true:
- [ ] Lochia has stopped or is spotting only
- [ ] Zero perineal soreness sitting on a hard chair for 10 minutes
- [ ] No heaviness or dragging after your longest walk
- [ ] You're past PP day 42 *or* your provider has said yes

Saddle pressure on a healing perineum is the reason for the wait — not the cardio.
If it's uncomfortable in the first 2 minutes, get off. A padded cover or a short-nose
saddle helps. Build 10 → 15 → 20 min over a week before adding any resistance.

## Phase 0 exit checklist — complete before Sep 28

- [ ] 30 min continuous walk, no heaviness, no bleeding change, no next-day soreness
- [ ] Pelvic floor 10-sec holds × 5, with a full release each time
- [ ] Bird dog and glute bridge with zero doming
- [ ] Lochia stopped
- [ ] 6-week visit **attended**, with these three things asked out loud:
  1. *"Am I cleared for progressive strength training and impact later?"*
  2. *"Can I have a referral to pelvic floor PT?"* — ask even if you feel fine
  3. *"Can I get a CBC, ferritin, TSH, and vitamin D?"* — fatigue at 6 weeks is not
     automatically the baby
- [ ] Intake survey filled in

Everything checked → **[Phase 1](phase-1-rebuild.md)** on Mon Sep 28.
Something unchecked → hold Phase 0 another week. It costs you one week now and saves
you two months later. This is the most common mistake and the most expensive one.
