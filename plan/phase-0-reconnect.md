## The three changes from the original plan

### 1. Walking is capped and split, not ramped

The original had you at 30 continuous minutes by Sep 26. Scrap that. Heaviness after
walking means walking volume is currently the load that exceeds your capacity, and
progressing it on schedule would make the symptom worse, not build tolerance.

- **Find your symptom-free duration.** Probably 10-15 min. Walk that, stop, and note it.
- **Split, don't extend.** Two or three 10-minute walks beat one 25-minute walk.
  Same total, far less accumulated downward load.
- **Progress only after 5 symptom-free days**, and only by 3-5 min on one walk.
- **After every walk: 5 minutes flat on your back, hips slightly elevated** on a
  cushion. Legs up the wall is even better. This is not optional — it is the
  reset that makes the next walk possible.
- **Ration standing time generally.** Heaviness is cumulative across the day, not
  per-session. Sit for feeds. Sit to fold laundry.

### 2. The bike becomes your cardio engine

Cycling is seated, non-weight-bearing, and puts almost no downward pressure through
the pelvic floor. It is close to the ideal cardio for exactly your symptom, and you
own one and have the shoes.

**You meet 3 of the 4 original gates:** lochia is spotting only ✓, stitches healed and
no perineal pain ✓, and sitting is comfortable ✓. The fourth was "past day 42" — you
are day 22. Your call, and worth a text to your provider before Sep 29, but 10 minutes
of easy pedaling with no saddle discomfort is a low-risk thing at 3 weeks with a healed
2nd degree tear. If sitting on the saddle is uncomfortable in the first two minutes,
get off; that is the actual test.

**Ramp:** 10 min → 12 → 15 → 20 by Sep 29. Low resistance, conversational, no standing
out of the saddle, no leaderboard. Peloton "Low Impact" and "Beginner" rides.

### 3. No loaded carries. At all, this phase.

Carries were the centerpiece of the original Phase 1. They are the single worst thing
for a pelvic floor reporting heaviness, and they come out until it clears.

**This includes real life.** You have an older child, and picking them up repeatedly is
almost certainly a bigger daily load than anything in this plan.

- Sit down and let them climb into your lap rather than lifting them
- When you must lift: **exhale first, lift on the exhale**, keep them close, no twisting
- Hand off the car seat where you can. Carry it in the crook of your elbow at your
  side, not out in front
- Say the words out loud to your husband: *for the next month, you lift the toddler.*
  He is already on board with protecting 30 minutes; this is cheaper than that

## The Reset — daily, ~12 min

The one adjustment from the standard version: **your release work matters as much as
your lift work.** Tailbone pain and hemorrhoids alongside heaviness suggest a pelvic
floor that may be gripping as well as under-supporting. Those coexist more often than
people expect, and a floor that can't let go can't generate a strong contraction either.
So do not out-kegel this — the PT eval will settle which one you're dealing with.

**1. 360° breathing — 10 breaths.** On your back, knees bent, hand on ribs. Inhale: ribs
widen sideways *and back*, belly softens, **pelvic floor releases downward.** Exhale:
everything returns without you jamming it in. Do not suck the belly in.

**2. Connection breath — 10 reps.** Inhale to release. Exhale: gently lift the pelvic
floor and feel the low belly draw in behind it. Hold 3 sec. **Fully release on the next
inhale, and take one extra breath there.** Effort 3/10, not 10/10.

**3. Pelvic floor sets — once daily, not more.**
- 10 quick flicks (1 sec on, 1 sec fully off)
- 5 long holds — start at 3 sec, add 1 sec per week
- **Then 5 deliberate releases:** exhale, let everything soften and widen, hold the
  release for 5 seconds. Most people skip this half. You specifically should not.

**4. Mobility — 1 round.** Weighted toward your actual complaints: neck, upper back,
tailbone.

| Movement | Dose | For |
|---|---|---|
| Cat-cow, slow | 8 | Spine, and it mobilizes the tailbone |
| Chest opener over a block, lengthwise along the spine | 60 sec | The feeding hunch |
| Thread-the-needle | 5/side | Thoracic rotation |
| **Chin tucks** | 10 | Neck — likely part of the headaches |
| **Upper trap + levator stretch** | 45 sec/side | Same |
| Glute bridge — exhale up | 10 | Glutes, and it decompresses the pelvic floor |
| Heel slides — low back stays quiet | 8/side | Deep core, no doming |
| **Deep squat sit / happy baby, supported** | 60 sec | Pelvic floor **release** |
| Wrist + thumb stretch | 30 sec/side | 12 feeds a day |

**5. After every walk: 5 min legs up the wall.** Counts as part of the Reset.

## Doming — the working rule

Your belly domes on head lift, getting out of bed, and lifting the car seat. That means
those three exceed what your midline can currently manage.

- **Get out of bed by rolling to your side and pushing up with your arm.** Every time.
- **Car seat: hinge at the hips, exhale as you lift, keep it close.** Better still,
  don't — leave it in the car and carry the baby.
- **In any exercise, shorten the range until the ridge disappears.** That shortened
  range is your current training limit, and it moves within weeks.
- Recheck the gap monthly. **Width matters less than whether you can generate tension
  across it** — a narrow gap you can't stiffen is worse than a wide one you can.
